# Methodology and model governance

## Problem framing

The application tests a fictional corporate payment agent acting under **pre-approved buyer instructions**. Independent policy controls determine whether the proposal could proceed in the mock system; anomaly scores are triage signals, not permissions. **No real bank, merchant, supplier or agent is connected.**

## Research objectives

1. Evaluate the recall of scripted unauthorised payment proposals by an independent policy gate.
2. Measure avoidable operational friction as the share of legitimate proposals held or blocked.
3. Compare a lightweight unsupervised anomaly detector against explicit permission controls, without confusing fraud detection with mandate validation.
4. Quantify the face value of held or blocked fictional unsafe proposals; never call this money recovered or losses averted.
5. Demonstrate session-limited revocation, mock idempotency, human review, audit export and transparent limitations.

## Synthetic ground truth

`src/payments.py` uses seed `20261116` to create 1,020 synthetic cases across thirteen scenario families. The `reference_unsafe` label is assigned from the generating scenario, independently of the policy outcome. A separate `reference_attack` flag distinguishes deliberately malicious scenario families from missing administrative prerequisites. Routine, high-value legitimate and unusual-hour legitimate scenarios are labelled safe; the latter two may be held by policy, testing false friction.

No financial institution, customer or supplier records are used. All beneficiary accounts begin with `DEMO-`.

## Rule engine, in sequence

- `BLOCK` unknown or inactive agent mandate, scripted invoice injection, unknown or out-of-scope supplier, duplicate invoice, invalid amount or above-limit payment, and mismatched verified beneficiary.
- `HOLD` supplier bank change awaiting independent validation, missing buyer approval, unusually high *in-limit* value or out-of-hours proposal.
- `ALLOW` only when the above triggers do not apply. This may produce a mock settlement record but never calls a payment provider.

`HOLD` and `BLOCK` cannot be released through human text entry alone; a reviewer writes an auditable fictional decision but does not alter the underlying payment facts. A session revocation can turn a previously allowed proposal into BLOCK. Session replay of mock settlements is prohibited by a set of completed proposal IDs.

## Model benchmark split and calibration

The 1,020 scripted cases are split with `train_test_split(..., stratify=scenario, test_size=0.25, random_state=20261116)` into **765 training** and **255 holdout** proposals. The Isolation Forest is fitted exclusively on ordinary benign training examples. A second benign-only training subset calibrates the anomaly threshold at the 95th percentile. The holdout never trains the model or selects its threshold. Model inputs include observable numeric and Boolean payment features but exclude scenario names, labels and policy outputs.

Isolation Forest is an unsupervised baseline, not a fraud classification product. It intentionally lacks the clear rule semantics of the independent policy gate.

**Crucial generalisation limitation:** Known scenario families occur in both train and test. This is an illustrative stratified holdout, *not an unseen-threat or independent external test*. The benchmark was designed around the same risks that motivated the policy rules. Therefore the high rule-based result is expected and cannot validate security in novel operational conditions.

## Metrics (holdout, not production)

- **Intervention recall**: Unsafe scripted cases whose outcome was `HOLD` or `BLOCK`, divided by all scripted unsafe cases.
- **Intervention precision**: Scripted unsafe cases among all policy interventions.
- **False hold rate**: Legitimate scripted cases that were held, divided by all legitimate cases.
- **Legitimate straight-through**: Legitimate scripted cases whose outcome was `ALLOW`, divided by all legitimate cases.
- **Fictional face value intercepted**: Sum of amounts on unsafe scripted proposals that reached HOLD/BLOCK. This is *not* verified fraud value, realised loss reduction or actual financial savings.

All numeric results are generated by `scripts/generate_artifacts.py` and stored in `artifacts/evaluation_manifest.json`. SHA-256 values help detect changed CSV bytes; they do not provide a signature or prove real payment correctness.

## Governance safeguards in the demo

The prototype illustrates assigned business ownership, static delegated payment scopes, independent runtime policy checking, simulated revocation, blocked invoice instruction overrides, discretionary holds, documented human review, browser-session audit export and transparent risk limitations. It does not implement multi-party signatures, production IAM, authenticated bank requests, payment tokenisation, real-time AML or sanctions checks, privileged access safeguards, security monitoring, auditable retention, operational resilience or regulatory compliance assessment.
